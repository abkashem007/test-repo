How to Spot a Supplement Scam in 5 Minutes

Five checks you can run on any product page before you enter a card number. No science degree needed.

[AU]By [Author Name]Updated [Date][X] min read

1. Find the full label

A legitimate product shows every ingredient and its dose. “Proprietary blend” with a single total weight means you can’t tell whether the key ingredient is there in a useful amount.

2. Compare doses with the research

Look up the main ingredient and check the dose used in studies. If the product uses a tenth of it, the study doesn’t apply.

3. Read the refund terms, not the badge

A “60-day guarantee” badge means little until you know whether opened bottles count, who pays return shipping and how to request a refund.

4. Walk the checkout

Watch for pre-ticked auto-ship boxes, “one-time offers” after payment and shipping fees that only show at the last step.

5. Search for complaints

Search “[product] refund” and “[product] complaint”. A handful of complaints is normal; a pattern of unrefunded charges is not.

Quick red flags: countdown timers that reset, “doctors hate this”, before/after photos with no source, and claims to cure a disease.
